    Data feasibility and an honest real-data study¶

    Evidence inspected on 2026-09-10. This is a feasibility assessment, not a finding of a profitable market strategy. Public accessibility, permission to redistribute, point-in-time availability, and executable trading history are four different questions.

    The recommended study is now implemented in the frozen experiment. This document retains the acquisition and study-design rationale; actual retrieval records, exclusions, measured scores, and reproduction commands live with that experiment rather than being implied by this earlier feasibility work.

    Decision¶

    Use a small, attributed NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory monthly Mauna Loa carbon-dioxide dataset for a reproducible, retrospective probability-forecasting study. Do not represent it as historical prediction-market data, an archive of issued forecasts, a reconstruction of information available to traders, or an executable-profit backtest.

    This audit did not establish a small, redistributable, complete point-in-time prediction-market order-book dataset. Official venue APIs are useful acquisition interfaces, but an interface is not a redistribution license and a historical price series is not a historical order book.

    Documentation: https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/data.html

    Use policy: https://gml.noaa.gov/about/disclaimer.html

This is a mutable current file, not a promised permanent historical version. The file's calendar records start in March 1958.     Field Type Meaning and caution
    year integer Observation year, not publication year.
    month integer, 1-12 Observation month, not an as-of timestamp.
    decimal_date decimal Source-provided fractional year; retain rather than infer availability from it.
    average decimal Monthly mean CO2, in parts per million.

    sdev decimal Standard deviation of days; negative values flag unavailable/interpolated information.
    uncertainty decimal Uncertainty of monthly mean; negative values flag unavailable/interpolated information.

    Keep the provider's original values and names in a raw schema; use the names above only in a documented normalized schema. Skip lines beginning with #, reject non-eight-field rows, reject duplicate or out-of-order months, validate numeric fields, and record all exclusions.

    Rights and attribution¶

    NOAA GML's policy states that government-server information is public domain unless specifically annotated otherwise; it permits public use subject to attribution, no implied NOAA endorsement, and no presentation of modified material as official government information. It separately warns about third-party material.

    The dataset header explicitly identifies March 1958-April 1974 as Scripps Institution of Oceanography data. Do not assume the NOAA government-work statement independently licenses those third-party records. Select January 1975-December 2025 for this book's compact NOAA-period study. This is a conservative provenance boundary, not a legal determination about Scripps data.

    Attribute the extracted observations to NOAA GML, Carbon Cycle and Greenhouse Gases group. Identify them as U.S. government data not covered by the book's original-content copyright. Label normalized data and derived targets as the book authors' transformations, not official NOAA products. Preserve the source URL, access time, source creation string, filtering rule, raw-content hash, extract hash, row count, and source citation in the data manifest. Consult the provider's current citation guidance; do not invent a dataset DOI or list the book authors as the measurement creators.

    Scientific and availability qualifications¶

    • This is a current-vintage retrospective series. The documentation says values can change following quality-control checks. A date-ordered split cannot recover historical vintages.
    • Monthly means are corrected to the center of the month using a seasonal cycle. Even the average column should not be assumed to be an untouched first release.
    • The header says missing months have been interpolated and, for NOAA data, negative standard deviation and uncertainty identify these cases. Do not use interpolated rows as if observed. Exclude targets or features depending on flagged months, and preserve calendar gaps rather than shifting the next retained row into the missing month's place.
    • Measurement at Mauna Loa was suspended after the November 2022 eruption and resumed in July 2023. The header identifies December 2022-July 4, 2023 observations as coming from Maunakea, approximately 21 miles away. Record this location discontinuity and report a sensitivity analysis excluding the affected period if performed.
    • The deseasonalized column is especially inappropriate as an allegedly as-of feature without vintage documentation. Its omission does not turn the remaining final-vintage study into a point-in-time study.
    • No source publication-time history, market contract, executable quote, fee history, or queue data has been established for these observations.

    Companion integration boundary¶

    Reuse the companion's scoring functions with explicitly constructed binary targets, but do not force this revised NOAA archive into EventRow by inventing publication/observation clocks or a market-probability benchmark. The companion data contract and public API guide describe the supported interfaces. A calendar-chronological retrospective split is not a verified point-in-time split. Keep point-in-time, execution-history and cost evidence unverified, and reject any inference of a supported live edge.

    Predeclared teaching question¶

    Can a simple seasonal probability model improve on an expanding overall event rate when predicting whether next month's recorded monthly mean exceeds this month's recorded monthly mean?

    This is a proposed forecasting study, not a statement that the implementation has already run or that a favorable result exists.

    1. Freeze the selected real-data extract before comparing model performance. Keep January 1975-December 2025 as the intended window; disclose actual exclusions and usable event count.
    2. Define one event per calendar target month: the recorded monthly mean is strictly greater than the immediately preceding month's mean. Ties count as NO. Only consecutive, valid monthly observations qualify.
    3. Use a fixed early chronological training period and later chronological evaluation period, or expanding-origin evaluation with a prespecified minimum training span. The experiment must state exact dates before inspecting competing scores.
    4. Compare an expanding overall rate with a month-of-year seasonal frequency model, using prespecified smoothing. If logistic regression is added, train all coefficients and preprocessing only on preceding rows and do not tune on evaluation outcomes.
    5. State that the information clock is a retrospective index convention, not a verified historical publication clock. A hypothetical one-month availability lag can illustrate disciplined coding, but cannot prove that these values were actually available then.
    6. Score one forecast per target month using the book's single-YES Brier score and natural-log loss. Show base rates, sample counts, probability ranges, and the paired score difference. Treat zero/one predictions explicitly.
    7. Dependence and seasonal structure matter. If uncertainty intervals are reported, document the dependence model and block length; do not bootstrap monthly rows independently and call that a universal guarantee. A descriptive paired score difference without an interval is acceptable.
    8. Report an unfavorable or inconclusive comparison unchanged. No P&L calculation belongs in the empirical output unless clearly separated as an invented contract illustration.

    The useful lesson is distinguishing strong recurring structure, credible forecast scoring, and unavailable trading evidence. This dataset does not test the relative quality of market prices, prove the transferability of a climate model to event contracts, or establish a tradable edge.

    Read-only venue acquisition: a separate future study¶

    The companion's classroom OrderBook, FeeSchedule and PaperBroker are not native venue representations. Their documented whole-unit quantities, four-decimal USD price/payout grid, and invented cent-rounded per-fill fees must not be confused with Kalshi fractional count_fp quantities or its order-level fee accumulator, nor with Polymarket collateral and market-specific fees. Optional public snapshot acquisition documented in data/README.md preserves native fields separately; a returned snapshot is not a verified conversion into the classroom broker. Actual quantity, collateral, rules, fee and execution adapters require separate validation.

    Kalshi¶

    Start from https://docs.kalshi.com/llms.txt, then inspect the current API-environment, historical-data, pagination, rate-limit, order-book, market-rule, and fee documentation. The inspected historical-data page partitions records using GET /historical/cutoff; market settlement and trade creation use different cutoff fields. Query the appropriate live and historical interfaces rather than assuming the live interface holds every past record.

    The inspected current order-book guide documents GET /markets/{ticker}/orderbook, with orderbook_fp.yes_dollars and orderbook_fp.no_dollars arrays. Each level contains string-valued dollar price and fixed-point quantity. The arrays contain bids; ordinary binary opposite-side bids imply asks. Preserve size and units. The current guide gives the public base root https://external-api.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2. Recheck these details before implementing a collector.

    The historical endpoints listed in the inspected guide cover markets, candlesticks, trades, and relevant account records. They do not establish a complete historical order-book or queue archive. Account fills are not a substitute for all traders' order histories. A candle or trade alone cannot establish that an unplaced resting order would have filled.

    Kalshi does have a separate mock-funds demo. Its official warning says demo price and behavior may not reflect real markets. Separate credentials and demo testing do not establish live execution fidelity.

    Polymarket¶

    Start from https://docs.polymarket.com/llms.txt. Identify the exact product and outcome-token IDs. The inspected prices/order-books documentation exposes public CLOB reads such as https://clob.polymarket.com/book?token_id=..., with price/size arrays, book timestamp, state hash, minimum order size, and tick size. Preserve source timestamps and independently recorded receive timestamps. A state hash identifies a returned book state; it is not proof of complete history.

    Do not assume zero fees. The inspected fee guide describes market-dependent taker fees and a price-dependent calculation. Its USDC fee descriptions coexist with a pUSD collateral/settlement guide: identify the actual unit and product rather than collapsing them into cash dollars. Resolution documentation permits an exceptional 50/50 payout and discusses rule clarifications after trading starts. Preserve rule versions and payout vectors rather than forcing every settlement into a binary label.

    The inspected polymarket.com/tos response displayed a U.S. trading block and a link to the separate polymarket.us product. It did not expose complete operative terms in the returned text. This is not a verified all-countries eligibility matrix or a legal opinion about the U.S. product.

    Permission gate and minimum archival schema¶

    No unrestricted venue-data redistribution license was established in this audit. The attempted Kalshi terms page returned a rate-limit response; complete operative Polymarket terms were not readable in the retrieved page. Unknown rights remain unknown. Do not redistribute venue payloads or assert that their public API availability supplies permission. Obtain and record the applicable current terms or permission before any substantial acquisition, sharing, or deployment. Never bypass geographic or account restrictions.

    For an authorized, read-only prospective collector, retain: venue and product; series/event/market/outcome IDs; contract wording and rules hash; collateral and payout convention; venue event time; source publication time where available; local receive time; schema/version; bid/ask levels with quantities; tick and quantity grids; fee parameters with effective times; trading status; pagination cursor and collection gaps; request/retry log; and final payout plus its publication/settlement times. Store no credentials in datasets.

    A future execution study additionally needs a defensible fill model, latency and cancellation records, queue assumptions, and a held-out evaluation protocol. Where these are missing, narrow the conclusion to observed forecasting or market-description evidence and retain a no-trade result.
