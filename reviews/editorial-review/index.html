    Substantive findings and resolutions¶

    Area Finding Resolution
    Chapters 1, 6, 20, 28 A fractional exceptional state was said necessarily to destroy complement pricing. Distinguished ordinary binary-label probabilities from full payoff vectors. A 0.50/0.50 pair still pays one unit in total. Added settlement-time, financing, collateral, and feasible-trading qualifications.
    Chapters 2-4 Odds direction was reversed; immediate taker execution was presented as all execution; Wolfers-Zitzewitz and Manski were assigned the same risk-neutrality premise. Corrected in-favor versus against odds, maker/taker distinctions, and the separate theoretical assumptions. Kept Berg's vote-share estimand and five-election limitation; removed any implied measured effective sample size.
    Chapter 5 Splitting an LMSR trade was said automatically to reduce its cost, contradicting path independence. A log identity was malformed. Derived telescoping split costs, repaired the stable log-sum-exp expression, required positive liquidity, and distinguished numerical safeguards from universal precision. Corrected fixed-point quantity strings and the loss-bound limiting argument.
    Chapter 6 and related claims USD 0.90 profit was called payout; a full-refund void appeared to be a documented general venue rule. Separated USD 5.00 receipts from USD 0.90 profit. Explicitly labeled refund conventions hypothetical and distinguished refunded fees from nonrefunded fees. Removed unsupported void attribution.
    Chapters 7-11 Different labels could be mistaken for independence; several passages required eventual labels at their own earlier forecast time. Distinguished dependence from contractual identity, feature decision cutoffs from fitting cutoffs, and legitimate future evaluation outcomes from leakage or target-vintage substitution. Corrected constructor, splitter, and evaluation-guard descriptions.
    Chapters 9-10 Fisher-transform uncertainty was described as raw correlation uncertainty, and dependence was said always to increase standard errors. Stated the transform and illustrative normal-theory assumptions; removed the universal variance-direction claim. Kept event and row counts separate.
    Chapter 10 The Brier uncertainty term was called an irreducible score floor; grouped forecasts were paired in the wrong order; the decomposition gap was attributed to the wrong term. Explained how resolution can offset uncertainty, corrected the grouped sequence, and derived the within-bin variance/covariance remainder. Distinguished sharpness from distance to a base rate.
    Chapters 10-12, 16, 19 Calibration fitting and evaluation cohorts were confused, and a worse toy score was presented as proof of a particular overfitting cause. Validation fits the map; test assesses it. Preserved the observed deterioration and qualified its possible explanation. Removed automatic-calibration recommendations.
    Chapters 11-12 Scaling, posterior inference, and boosting implementation were overstated. Explained scaling's penalty/conditioning role, posterior mode versus full posterior, and raw-gradient teaching updates versus the installed boosted classifier's initialization and leaf updates. Removed an unsupported learning-rate-times-stage score bound.
    Chapter 13 Minimum-variance linear averaging was said to require Gaussian unbounded variables and to leave the probability interval even with valid convex inputs. Derived the variance minimizer without that assumption and separated linear estimation from a Gaussian posterior model. Distinguished discrete hazard probabilities, continuous rates, event occurrence, and settlement delay.
    Chapters 14-15 Hand TF-IDF and sigmoid-network calculations were presented too close to literal descriptions of different installed defaults. Documented smoothed IDF, raw counts and L2 normalization; separated sigmoid hand derivations from the six-unit ReLU companion. Corrected feature-vector ordering and optimizer guarantees.
    Chapter 16 Minimum variance was equated with minimum Brier without a bias assumption; unconstrained weights were treated as probability weights; a blend could supposedly be worse than both components. Added zero-mean/second-moment conditions, convex constraints and degeneracy handling. Explained the convex-loss bound and separate weight-fitting/evaluation cohorts.
    Chapters 17, 20, 28 Ceiling notation omitted cent units; basket examples mislabeled flat-dollar fees as notional percentages. Defined cent ceiling, corrected fee units and checked both conventions. Aligned required_cash with the larger of modeled cost and summed reservations, without calling rounding a venue price tick.
    Chapter 20 Payoff-dominance trade direction and executable inequality were reversed. The nonnegative-payoff trade buys broad and sells narrow; strict cash-positive opportunity requires narrow bid above broad ask, under feasible shorting/creation assumptions. The companion remains long-only.
    Chapters 18, 24 Seven clocks were headed “six”; partial execution was confused with full-size screening failure; cancellation requests were conflated with effective cancellation. Corrected the count and distinct interfaces. Explicitly separated ordered paper replay from asynchronous venue match/cancel/receipt reconciliation.
    Chapter 21 Arrival intensities were equated to finite-window probabilities; expected loss and commitment limits were treated as maximum session-loss bounds. Recast the worked example as a coherent one-opportunity probability model, retaining its checked numbers. Distinguished payout risk, administrative settlement, outstanding commitments, and cumulative session loss.
    Chapter 22 Fee symbol f was reused for Kelly allocation; nonlinear costs were handled by plugging an average cost into a constant-cost optimum. Used phi in math notation, proved concavity, and derived the quantity-level log-wealth objective for nonlinear costs. Kept it explicitly unimplemented. Distinguished cash loss, payout notional, borrowing, and optional sizing objectives.
    Chapter 23 IPS missing finite-sample matches were described as undefined weights; timing and unbiasedness conditions were incomplete. Distinguished zero population propensity from an absent sampled match, documented the stricter code gate, and stated fixed-policy, logging, reward-consistency, and fitting-time conditions. Qualified weight ESS.
    Chapter 25 Correct equal-weight convex projection was said able to worsen aggregate squared error on a feasible realized outcome vector. Added the projection inequality and its narrow loss guarantee, while preserving empirical questions about calibration, incorrect edges, weights, coverage, execution, and genuine novelty.
    Chapters 26-30 A version lock was called hash-locked; fixed source code was treated as evidence of predeclaration; results were described as frozen before scoring. The proposed ask-only edit made a crossed book. Corrected environment and evidence claims, labeled output excerpts, and supplied a valid bid/ask sensitivity edit. Corrected basket error semantics, synthetic round-trip scope, and actual evidence-flag values. The manual-only closing boundary is unchanged.
    Claim fragments Several rows retained obsolete metadata-only descriptions, misclassified abstract inspection, or repeated corrected source/technical claims. Reconciled 14 rows in six fragment files with the existing audit's recorded depth and corrected manuscript. No source record was upgraded or new primary-text reading invented.

    Mathematical and numerical work¶

    The canonical USD accounting remained unchanged: cost 4.10, binary profits 5.90 and minus 4.10, model-conditional EV 1.40, resale profit 0.40, and fractional-settlement profit 0.90 with no binary label.

    Independent in-memory calculations checked:

    • LMSR ten-unit cost 6.201145 at liquidity 10, 5.124948 at liquidity 100, and equality of one ten-unit trade with successive four- and six-unit trades.
    • Raw/grouped Brier values 0.075/0.0625, zero perfect-forecast loss, and infinite wrong-certainty natural-log loss.
    • Logistic probability and both log-loss branches, the two-forecaster weight 0.272727 and variance 0.031818, and the three feasible outcome-vector projection comparisons.
    • The sigmoid hand network's forward pass and all nine parameter gradients, with central finite differences agreeing within 1e-8.
    • Fill-conditioned probabilities approximately 0.461078, 0.401993, and 0.328358, with ten-unit expected profits approximately 0.510778, minus 0.080066, and minus 0.816418.
    • Quarter-Kelly fraction 0.059322 under the stated constant-cost assumptions.
    • Flat-fee basket totals 10.10/9.90/9.82 versus notional-rate totals 9.96/9.86/9.82. The 0.3333-price regression case costs 2.02 but requires 2.10, rejecting the lower funding.
    • Chapter 27's original suggested ask-only modification raises crossed book is ambiguous; the corrected bid 0.14/ask 0.15 case returns a paper candidate with cost 1.60 and modeled profit 0.405420.
    • The normal-noise selection illustration: mean maxima about 1.867827 for 20 draws and 1.161329 for five; repeated-look false positives 24.8105% versus 5.032% at the single final look under the disclosed construction.
