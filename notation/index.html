    Notation and accounting conventions¶

    The same symbol should mean the same thing across a forecast, a simulation, and a trade log. This registry is part of the book's contract with the reader.

    Symbol Meaning
    \(E\) An explicitly defined event, with its contractual resolution rules.
    \(Y\) A binary contractual outcome, zero or one. It is not necessarily identical to an informal description of the real-world event.
    \(R\) Actual terminal payout per unit, in the specified collateral currency. In the ordinary USD 1 binary example, \(R=Y\); exceptional settlement can differ.
    \(p\) A conditional probability under a stated model or information set; not an observable oracle for the true event chance.
    \(\hat p\) An estimated forecast of the contractual YES probability.
    \(p_L\) A deliberately conservative probability bound or scenario, with its inference method stated. It is not automatically a valid confidence bound.
    \(a\), \(b\) Executable ask and bid per unit, respectively, for a particular outcome and size.
    \(m\) A specified market forecasting benchmark, often the midpoint when fresh and meaningful. It is not an executable price.
    \(Q\) Number of contract units. Classroom examples use whole units unless otherwise stated; venues can impose different quantity grids.
    \(f\) Hypothetical per-unit transaction cost in the simplest classroom calculations. Real fee functions can depend on price, size, side, time, and rounding.
    \(F(Q,a)\) An explicitly specified total fee function for an order or fill.
    \(\Pi\) Net profit or loss, with currency, time horizon, and realized/unrealized status stated.
    \(W\) Bankroll or equity, with a clear distinction between available cash and collateral reserved for positions or orders.
    \(x\) Feature vector known by the relevant decision cutoff.
    \(t\) A timestamp or time index whose meaning is explicitly specified.
    \(n\) Number of evaluation observations; report the number of independent or dependence-grouped events separately.
    \(\lambda\) A regularization or penalty parameter, defined locally.
    \(\beta\) Statistical-model coefficients.
    \(B\) LMSR liquidity parameter, to avoid confusing it with the executable bid \(b\).
    \(\phi\) Fraction of bankroll spent on a position in Chapter 22; distinct from per-unit fee \(f\).
    \(\kappa\) Fraction of the full log-growth-optimal spend used in a fractional-Kelly illustration.

    Modeling chapters explicitly introduce additional local symbols. In LMSR, \(q_i\) counts cumulative shares sold, not probabilities; \(\pi_i\) denotes its marginal price. Superscripted neural-network \(b^{(1)}_j\) denotes a bias, not a quoted bid. Chapter 23's bandit \(b(a\mid s)\) is a behavior-policy probability and its local \(R\) is net reward, not this registry's terminal payout. Keep these qualified local uses distinct from unqualified trading symbols.

    A convex average of valid probabilities remains a valid probability, but need not preserve calibration. Under Brier or log loss on the same cases, convexity bounds the blend's loss by the weighted average component loss; it does not guarantee beating the best component.

    A common worked example¶

    Buy 10 YES units at USD 0.40 with an illustrative flat fee of USD 0.01 per unit. These are invented teaching costs, not a Kalshi or Polymarket fee schedule.

    \[ \text{initial cost}=10(0.40+0.01)=4.10. \]

    Under ordinary binary settlement, the terminal payout is USD 10 if YES and USD 0 if NO. Net profit is therefore USD 5.90 or minus USD 4.10. There is no exit fee in this hold-to-settlement example unless a separately stated rule introduces one.

    If the model assigns \(\hat p=0.55\), its modeled expected net profit is

    \[ \mathbb{E}_{\hat p}[\Pi]=10(0.55-0.40-0.01)=1.40. \]

    The subscript matters: this is an expectation under a forecast that might be wrong, not evidence that the trader possesses an edge. It says nothing about the next realized outcome by itself.

    If instead all units can actually be sold at a bid of USD 0.46 with an additional USD 0.01-per-unit exit fee, realized round-trip profit is

    \[ 10(0.46-0.40-0.01-0.01)=0.40. \]

    Do not count the spread a second time after already using the ask to buy and bid to sell. Slippage is an additional difference between a specified reference executable quote and the realized price, not an invitation to subtract the same cost twice.

    Forecast scores¶

    For one YES forecast per binary evaluation observation, the Brier score is

    \[ \operatorname{BS}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\hat p_i-y_i)^2. \]

    Some multiclass conventions sum both YES and NO errors and give twice this value. This book uses the single-YES convention. Log loss uses natural logarithms:

    \[ \operatorname{LL}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \left[y_i\log\hat p_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-\hat p_i)\right]. \]

    A probability of zero on an outcome that occurs incurs infinite loss. Numerical stabilization, when used in fitting, must be disclosed rather than retrospectively altering an evaluated forecast. A perfectly confident correct forecast contributes zero, using the limiting convention for the otherwise undefined zero-times-log-zero expression.

    An average over observations is not automatically an average over independent events. Specify whether scoring is per event, horizon, contract, or timestamp and why the weighting answers the research question.

    Time and availability¶

    Use timezone-aware UTC. Keep at least these concepts distinct: event time, source publication time, local observation time, decision time, fill time, outcome-label availability time, and final settlement time. Historical revisions must not be treated as information available before their publication.

    A forecast model fitted at a past cutoff may use only labels available at that cutoff. A chronological split of rows can still leak a shared outcome across training and evaluation. Event grouping and time order are joint constraints, not alternatives.

    Costs, currencies, and statistical language¶

    Price and payout units must match. A token redeemable for a collateral asset is not a promise that the collateral has no custody, conversion, or exchange-rate risk. Clearly identify any USD, stablecoin, token, or other collateral example.

    "Expected profit" means a model-conditional expectation. "Realized profit" requires an actual or explicitly simulated fill and recognized payout or sale. "Marked profit" depends on a disclosed valuation convention. "Statistically supported edge" requires a specified estimand, data, dependence model, uncertainty procedure, and protection against selection; it is not established by an attractive point estimate.

    For a trade that can be lost in full, affordable loss is a prerequisite, not a mathematical guarantee of safety. No universal confidence level, sample count, Kelly fraction, or paper-trading duration removes model risk.
